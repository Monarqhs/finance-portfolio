# 01. Vision & Scope

## Status

Draft — awaiting review. Contract-first: this document is written **before** any
implementation. No code exists until the specs in `docs/02-analysis-specs/` are
approved.

## Background

The predecessor project `finance-weekly-report` sends a weekly portfolio digest
by email. It is well engineered (zero-dependency Python, fail-safe fetching) but
its analysis is **purely technical price tracking**: SMA50/SMA200, RSI-14, and
distance from the 52-week high, turned into BUY / HOLD / CAUTION labels.

The owner wants more than tracking. The goal of `finance-portfolio` is a
**personal investment analyst** that emails a weekly report and reasons about
the portfolio, the market, and the world — not just the price chart.

## Vision

> A weekly email from a personal analyst that knows my real positions, reads the
> market and the macro backdrop, understands the conflicts driving it, gives me
> an unbiased read with evidence and limitations, and actively surfaces
> diversification opportunities — all on free tiers.

## Goals (what this project IS)

1. **Know my real numbers.** Positions, average cost, realized/unrealized P&L,
   and the **USD/IDR currency effect** — derived from a real transaction ledger,
   not static weights.
2. **Know the market.** Prices and technicals (Yahoo), plus macro context
   (FRED): Treasury yields, the yield curve, Fed policy, the US dollar.
3. **Give unbiased advice.** A rule-based signal engine, guarded against bias:
   every conclusion carries its **evidence and its limitations**, and the system
   never predicts prices.
4. **Understand current conflicts.** A news/geopolitics layer feeds a narrative
   analyst (LLM) that reasons from **dated evidence** we supply — with a
   deterministic fallback when the LLM is disabled.
5. **Surface sector-ETF opportunities.** An opportunity scanner evaluates sector
   ETFs for healthy trend and low correlation to semiconductors, and proposes
   diversification candidates.

## Non-goals (what this project is NOT)

- **Not a price predictor.** No forecasting of future prices or returns. The
  analyst explains *what is happening and why*, with evidence, not *what will
  happen*.
- **Not a trading bot.** It never places orders. It informs a human.
- **Not professional financial advice.** Output is informational; the owner
  makes all decisions.
- **Not a web app (for now).** Delivery is the weekly email digest. An
  interactive dashboard is explicitly deferred to a later phase.
- **Not a data store inside an LLM.** The LLM is stateless; the owner's data
  lives in the owner's own systems (see Architecture).

## Design principles (inherited from the `rea` project)

- **Evidence-first.** Every signal or recommendation states the data behind it
  and what it cannot conclude. "SMH is down 4%, 10Y yield rose to X%, RSI still
  healthy → macro rotation, not a broken trend" — with the numbers attached.
- **Fail-safe.** A failed data source degrades gracefully; the report still
  ships with a clear note, never a crash.
- **Auditable over clever.** Rule-based and reproducible beats opaque. The LLM
  adds narrative on top of a deterministic core, never replaces it.

## Privacy posture (locked decision)

- The transaction ledger lives in the **owner's own Google Sheet**, not in the
  repo and not in any LLM.
- Python assembles the **full report** (with real IDR amounts) and emails it to
  the owner.
- The LLM receives **only a de-identified view** — structure and percentages,
  never real amounts, account names, or any PII. Real numbers never leave the
  owner's own systems toward a third party.
- The repository is currently **public**. No secrets, no Sheet IDs, and no real
  amounts are ever committed; everything sensitive goes through GitHub Secrets.
  Making the repository private is recommended but left to the owner.

## Delivery & cadence

- **Weekly email digest** (same channel as the predecessor), scheduled via
  GitHub Actions.
- Optional **reactive alerts** (e.g. a sharp yield move or holding drawdown) are
  a candidate for a later iteration, still as a scheduled batch job.

## Success criteria

The project succeeds when the weekly email answers, with evidence:

1. What do I actually hold, and what is my P&L including the currency effect?
2. Is this week's move explained by macro rotation, or by something breaking in
   my holdings' fundamentals?
3. Where am I over-concentrated, and which sector ETFs could reduce that risk?
4. What current conflicts/events are relevant to my positions, and why?
